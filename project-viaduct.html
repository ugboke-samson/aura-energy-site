Project Reference: CIV-098 • Infrastructure Development

Centennial Interlink Viaduct

Comprehensive engineering blueprint, geological survey analysis, and long-span transit corridor design metrics.

1. Executive Project Proposal

The Centennial Interlink Viaduct proposal addresses structural transit constraints within active deepwater shipping routes. The mandate required the erection of a 4.2-mile pre-stressed concrete elevated highway network to integrate global maritime corridors with municipal transit grids. The primary objective demanded zero interference with high-volume cargo routing timelines throughout the structural assembly timeline.

2. Civil Engineering & Foundations

Rayton engineering divisions deployed custom deep structural piling layouts down to solid bedrock strata horizons at depths exceeding 80 meters. Due to soft alluvial delta topsoils, high-torque hydraulic rotary rigs cleared underwater paths lined with heavy steel reinforcement casing. Massive concrete footings were poured using high-performance self-consolidating aggregate mixtures, establishing absolute vertical resistance parameters against cross-current marine shear forces.

3. Structural Superstructure Alignment

The spans rely on an advanced pre-stressed and post-tensioned composite concrete box girder system. Heavy modular segments were cast in controlled environment yards and hoisted using specialized marine crane assemblies during low-tide windows. Internal high-tensile steel tendon webs were tensioned to strict force tolerances using precision hydraulic jacks, creating strong wide-span clearing lines with minimal mid-channel columns.

4. Telemetry & Continuous Monitoring

To preserve long-term asset life, fiber-optic structural strain sensors were embedded inside the main concrete beams during production. These sensors feed continuous telemetry to our central data systems, monitoring expansion loop tolerances, wind load displacements, and localized live load distributions in real time.
